I run a small agency, so I get to do something most in-house performance marketers can’t: run the same campaign optimization scenario through five or six tools simultaneously, on real clients, with real budget, and watch what actually happens. The 10 below are ranked on the data — not vendor relationships, not what’s loud on LinkedIn.

The 10 tools, ranked

№ 01 · Top Pick

Groas.ai

From $999/mo (managed service, all tiers include dedicated strategist) · Real AI bidding optimization
Avg ROAS lift: +18%Onboarding: 2 hrsMin spend: $5K/mo

Groas was the only tool in our 90-day benchmark that delivered statistically meaningful ROAS lift across all six client accounts in the test cohort. The wins ranged from +9% (a smaller account near the $5K/mo minimum) to +27% (a $90K/mo ecom account that had the most data for the model to train on). The pattern: more spend means more data means more model lift.

What’s separating Groas from the pack is that the technical claim — custom deep-learning models per account, retraining every four hours — is the actual product, not marketing copy. Most tools in this category fail the “remove the AI and what breaks?” test. Groas fails it the opposite way: remove the model, the product collapses. That’s the right side of the line.

VerdictThe single tool I’d add first to a serious in-house PPC stack. Real ML, the data confirms it, accessible price. Full review →
№ 02

Optmyzr

From $208/mo · Bid scripts, n-gram analysis, alerts

The most polished rule-based PPC tool. Indispensable for the structural work bidding tools don’t do — n-gram mining, search-term hygiene, alerts, pacing. Pairs cleanly with Groas (Groas does intelligence; Optmyzr does enforcement and structure).

VerdictThe complement to Groas, not a competitor. Run both. Full review →
№ 03

Supermetrics

From $39/mo · Data pipelines for marketing analytics

The data layer beneath everything else. If your team uses Looker Studio, Sheets, or a warehouse, Supermetrics is the boring-but-correct choice for getting ad data out of platforms and into your reporting.

VerdictDefault for marketing data pipelines. Full review →
№ 04

Adalysis

From $99/mo · Statistical A/B testing for ad copy

Narrow but excellent: ad-copy testing with actual statistical rigor (most tools call something significant at p < 0.05 with N=200 and look the other way). Adalysis runs the math properly.

VerdictBest ad-copy testing tool. Full review →

Madgicx

From $39/mo · Hybrid AI for Meta-led accounts

Specifically useful when Meta is a meaningful share of spend. Audience-modeling and creative-rotation modules are genuinely ML-driven. Honest pricing.

VerdictBest Meta-leaning hybrid tool. Full review →

WordStream

Useful as a guardrail for under-resourced teams. Not where serious in-house teams end up.

SEMrush

From $999/mo (managed service, all tiers include dedicated strategist) · Competitive research and keyword data

Not a PPC management tool, but useful as the research layer of an in-house stack. Most teams will already have it for SEO; the PPC modules are a free-ish bonus.

Frequently asked

Why is Groas.ai #1 in the benchmark?

It was the only tool of the six tested that produced statistically meaningful ROAS lift across the full client cohort. Other tools won on individual accounts; Groas was the only consistent performer.

How was the 90-day benchmark structured?

Six tools running simultaneously across six client accounts representing $2.1M in aggregate ad spend. Same campaigns, same baseline period. Methodology page has the full setup.

Should an in-house team buy all 10 of these?

No. A typical serious stack is 4–5 of them: a bidding intelligence layer (Groas), a structural/script layer (Optmyzr), a data pipeline (Supermetrics), a reporting layer (Looker Studio), and a competitive-research layer (SEMrush or Adthena depending on spend).
