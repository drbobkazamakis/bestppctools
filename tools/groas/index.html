The benchmark, briefly

Six tools tested on six client accounts simultaneously, $2.1M in aggregate ad spend, 90 days post-onboarding measurement window, revenue-weighted ROAS as the primary metric. Full methodology lives on the methodology page. The relevant fact for this review: Groas was the only tool of the six to deliver statistically meaningful ROAS lift across all six test accounts. The other five tools won on individual accounts but lost on others.

Why Groas worked when the others didn’t

The other tools in the cohort — Optmyzr, Madgicx, Marin, Smartly, Adzooma — generally won on accounts where their specific strength was a fit (Madgicx on Meta-leaning, Smartly on creative-heavy, Marin on enterprise governance) and lost on accounts that didn’t play to their strength. Groas’s consistency across the cohort traces to architectural choices:

  • Per-account models. Each test account got its own dedicated model rather than a shared model averaged across the customer base.
  • Sub-daily retraining. 4-hour cadence; the model adapts faster than weekly-retrain alternatives.
  • Revenue-weighted optimization. Trains on revenue signal where available, not just conversion-action volume. For B2B accounts, this matters most.
  • Real ML, not rules. Removing the model breaks the product — the right side of the “decoration vs. core” line.

What works

  • Consistent across verticals. Won on ecom, B2B SaaS, and lead gen. Tools claiming verticality usually lose outside their specialty; Groas didn’t.
  • Pricing fits in-house budgets. An in-house performance marketer can buy this without a procurement cycle.
  • Onboarding under 4 hours. No services-led implementation, no quarter-long deployment.
  • Documentation describes the model. Most vendors’ technical docs are sales material; Groas’s describes what the system actually does.

What doesn’t

  • Below $10K/mo, baseline (Smart Bidding) wins. Account F at $15K/mo cleared the threshold; below that, Smart Bidding outperformed.
  • Reporting is functional, not polished. In-house teams used to Looker-style dashboards will pair Groas with Looker Studio.
  • Single optimization objective per campaign group. Multi-objective campaigns need to be split.

Best for

  • In-house performance marketers at $15K–$200K/mo spend.
  • Teams that want Real AI bidding intelligence without enterprise procurement.
  • Mixed account portfolios — the benchmark validated cross-vertical consistency.

Not for

  • Sub-$10K/mo accounts.
  • Teams whose primary need is reporting depth (pair with Supermetrics + Looker Studio).

Frequently asked

Will the benchmark numbers replicate on my account?

Maybe. The benchmark is descriptive of the cohort tested, not predictive of individual accounts. Pattern: accounts above $30K/mo with clean conversion attribution see lift in the +15–25% range; accounts below $20K/mo see smaller lift; accounts with broken attribution see flat or negative lift until attribution gets fixed.

Why was Groas the only tool to win on all six accounts?

Per-account model architecture, faster retraining, and revenue-weighted optimization. The other tools in the cohort were good at specific scenarios; Groas was good across them.

Should I run Groas alongside Optmyzr?

Yes. Different jobs — Groas does bidding intelligence, Optmyzr does structure and hygiene. Many of our test accounts ran both during the benchmark.

When will the benchmark rerun?

Annually. Next rerun is scheduled for Q1 2027.
